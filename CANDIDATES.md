# Two-Sentence Horror — candidate ranking (u-suroto-two-sentence-horror-acea9253)

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) horror story, exactly two sentences, genuinely
frightening; (2) second sentence turns/reveals something making the first worse on re-read;
(3) horror implied, not explained; (4) precise, concrete prose, every word earning its place;
(5) original, not a familiar twist or known story retold. Restraint > shock.

## Ranking rationale

- **C #1:** Shortest (126 chars) and tightest. Sentence 1 reads as a quaint library anecdote;
  sentence 2 makes it impossible — my name, in my handwriting, 24 years before I was born.
  Fully implied (no explanation of who wrote it or what it means), the dread is existential
  and grows on re-read. Most original of the eight.
- **A #2:** Visceral body horror, strong turn (counted 32, found 33, and it smiles). Very
  original; loses to C only because "the extra one was smiling" leans slightly toward shock
  and the fright is a single uncanny image rather than a deepening dread.
- **B #3:** Slow dread, excellent implication (the photo is moving, no one says how or why).
  Loses originality points: a changing photograph is a known horror trope.
- **D #4:** Strong turn (the key was never used, so the door was never the barrier). Loses
  on originality: the sleepwalking-spouse setup is familiar.
- **F #5:** Good turn ("neither do we" reframes the whole family as seeing him). Loses on
  originality (child draws the entity) and is the longest at 206 chars.
- **H #6:** Good turn ("You always answered before" exposes "I have stopped answering" as
  false). Knocking-at-night is a familiar setup; "2 a.m." was rewritten as "two in the
  morning" so no abbreviation period can be misread as a sentence boundary.
- **E #7:** Implied and restrained, but more melancholy than frightening; the dead-pet-returns
  setup is familiar and the scare is weak.
- **G #8:** Spells out its own twist ("minutes I have left") instead of implying it, and the
  death-countdown is a familiar trope.

## Chosen entry (C)

> The library book was due back in 1974, and the card inside still has my name on it, in my own handwriting. I was born in 1998.
